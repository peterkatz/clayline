    04

    Interior

    What the nozzle lays inside the wall. You choose it, and nothing here chooses for you: Vase mode has to be off before Solid or Infill can be picked at all. Choosing one does clear what a filled interior cannot print beside — Bottom layers, and profile blend if a loaded pattern carried it. Each says so where it lives.

    Hollow prints the wall and nothing inside it — what Weave has always done.

    A solid or infill interior needs discrete layers. Turn Vase mode · spiral rise off to fill the interior.

    This pattern was fitted with profile blend, which rides the wall up and down in Z while a filled interior lays its fill flat. Profile blend is off for this print; choose Hollow to have it back.

    Soliddense on every layer

    Fills every layer densely and welds it to that layer's wall — for small functional pieces like a teapot lid. A solid piece is heavy; the report's wet weight is the honest number before you mix the clay.

    Crossing alternates the middle layers 45° and 135°, which bonds stronger. Spiral rings every layer instead, and reads as thrown pottery. Either way the bed face and the show face are spiralled.

    Infillsparse continuous ribs

    Sparse continuous ribs so a big piece dries as thin, even walls instead of cracking the way solid clay would. Every ribbed layer repeats the same ribs, so those beads stack into continuous internal walls — the base, cap and ramp layers you set below are dense or tightening instead, by your own choice.

    Lines run straight across the form at one angle, turning around on the wall so each rib welds to it. Concentric follows the wall inward in nested rings; its ring-to-ring step stacks into one internal ridge. Under a cap that ridge is sealed inside the piece; on a form left open at the top it is part of the interior surface you will look down into.

    Slice the form to read rib spacing — the sliced coil width sets it.
